# THE HUNT

> A one-minute cinematic short built through 3D previsualization, virtual cinematography and generative filmmaking.

## Overview

**THE HUNT** follows three defeated soldiers fleeing through a cold, rain-soaked forest. In the fog behind them, an unknown armored figure draws closer without a word.

The film was designed as a continuous cinematic sequence rather than a collection of independently generated shots. Its screenplay, geography, character movement and camera positions were established before generative production through a unified Blender previs.

| Runtime | Shots | World size | Previs length |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| ~1 min | 24 | 90 × 110 m | ~66 sec |

The central production question was simple: **can 3D previs give generative filmmaking stronger spatial continuity, character consistency and directorial control?**

## 3D World Blocking

The story takes place inside one shared environment. A **90 × 110 m World Master Scene** established the geography, movement paths, combat zones and encounter distances used by all 24 shots.

### Spatial System

- **90 × 110 m** forest environment
- **313** tree proxies
- **20 × 28 m** central encounter area
- Continuous movement paths for four characters
- **15 m** final confrontation distance
- **41.36 m** final escape route into the fog

## Camera Blocking

Once the geography was fixed, the story was staged as a complete 24-shot camera plan inside the same environment.

Framing, lens direction, screen direction and character positions were designed before final generation. This made it possible to track the relationship between the fleeing soldiers and the Knight throughout the sequence.

## Blender Previsualization

The world layout and camera plan were assembled in Blender as a full-film previs.

The renders were never intended as final imagery. They were working references for composition, camera movement, character movement, timing and continuity.

![Blender Hierarchy](THE%20HUNT/04_PREVIS/03_BLENDER/BLENDER_HIERARCHY.png)

The finished previs contained approximately **66 seconds of continuous cinematic structure**, allowing the pacing and spatial logic of the entire film to be reviewed before final shot generation.

> **The previs was a directing tool, not the final visual output.**
